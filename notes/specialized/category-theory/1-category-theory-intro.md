# Category Theory: Pre-orders, meets and joins

*Published: September 2, 2026*
*Category: Category Theory*

A [pre-order](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Preorder) is the smallest kind of [category](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Category_(mathematics)) — at most one arrow between any two objects. It is enough to define products, coproducts, and logic gates. This note walks through those constructions on two examples: the divisors of $12$ and the two-element set $\{F, T\}$.

<div class="intuition"><strong>Intuition.</strong> A pre-order is a category with at most one arrow $a \to b$, which exists iff $a \le b$. Once this is accepted, "product" and "coproduct" reduce to $\min$ and $\max$ under the order.</div>

---

## Pre-order

A [pre-order](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Preorder) on a set $P$ is a binary relation $\le$ that is

- [reflexive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reflexive_relation): $a \le a$ for every $a$;
- [transitive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transitive_relation): $a \le b$ and $b \le c$ imply $a \le c$.

A [partial order](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partially_ordered_set) additionally requires [antisymmetry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antisymmetric_relation): $a \le b$ and $b \le a$ imply $a = b$.

### Example: divisors of $12$

Take $P = \{1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 12\}$ with $a \le b$ iff $a$ [divides](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Divisor) $b$. Then $(P, \mid)$ is a partial order.

<details class="bulb">
<summary>Check reflexivity and transitivity</summary>

- Reflexive: $a \mid a$ because $a = 1 \cdot a$.
- Transitive: if $a \mid b$ and $b \mid c$, write $b = ka$ and $c = mb$. Then $c = (mk)a$, so $a \mid c$.

</details>

## Hasse diagram

Transitivity implies most $\le$-arrows for free. A [Hasse diagram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hasse_diagram) draws only the [cover relations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Covering_relation): pairs $a < b$ with no $c$ strictly between.

![Hasse diagram of the divisors of 12](assets/notes/specialized/category-theory/hasse-divisors12.svg)

*Generated by [`hasse_diagram.py`](https://github.com/TensorTheorist/TensorTheorist.github.io/blob/main/scripts/notes/specialized/category-theory/hasse_diagram.py).*

The diagram is read bottom-up. Any $a \le b$ is recovered by walking upward from $a$ to $b$ along the edges.

## Meets and joins

For $a, b \in P$:

- The [meet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Join_and_meet) $a \wedge b$ is the largest element $\le a$ and $\le b$.
- The [join](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Join_and_meet) $a \vee b$ is the smallest element $\ge a$ and $\ge b$.

On the divisor pre-order, $a \wedge b = \gcd(a, b)$ and $a \vee b = \operatorname{lcm}(a, b)$.

<details class="bulb">
<summary>Compute $4 \wedge 6$ and $4 \vee 6$</summary>

- $4 \wedge 6 = \gcd(4, 6) = 2$: the largest divisor of both $4$ and $6$.
- $4 \vee 6 = \operatorname{lcm}(4, 6) = 12$: the smallest multiple of both $4$ and $6$.

</details>

<div class="intuition"><strong>Intuition.</strong> In category-theoretic language the meet is the <a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Product_(category_theory)" target="_blank">product</a> and the join is the <a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coproduct" target="_blank">coproduct</a>. In a pre-order they collapse to $\min$ and $\max$ under the order.</div>

## The Boolean pre-order

Let $\mathbb{B} = \{F, T\}$ (the [Booleans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boolean_algebra)) with

$$F \le T.$$

Reflexivity gives $F \le F$ and $T \le T$; transitivity adds nothing. This is the whole pre-order.

## AND and OR from meets and joins

Compute $a \wedge b$ and $a \vee b$ for every pair $(a, b) \in \mathbb{B}^2$.

- $a \wedge b$: the largest element $\le a$ and $\le b$. Equal to $T$ only when both are $T$. This is [AND](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logical_conjunction).
- $a \vee b$: the smallest element $\ge a$ and $\ge b$. Equal to $T$ when at least one is $T$. This is [OR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logical_disjunction).

![AND and OR truth tables computed as meet and join in the Boolean pre-order](assets/notes/specialized/category-theory/and-or-tables.svg)

*Generated by [`truth_tables.py`](https://github.com/TensorTheorist/TensorTheorist.github.io/blob/main/scripts/notes/specialized/category-theory/truth_tables.py).*

<div class="intuition"><strong>Intuition.</strong> AND and OR are not primitives handed to a logic engine; they are the meet and join in the pre-order with a single non-trivial arrow $F \to T$.</div>

### Wiring the gates

```
AND(a, b) := a ∧ b     // meet
OR (a, b) := a ∨ b     // join
NOT a     := (a ⇒ ⊥)   // exponential to bottom, next note
```

$(\mathbb{B}, \wedge, \vee)$ is a [bounded lattice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lattice_(order)) — a pre-order with all finite meets and joins.
